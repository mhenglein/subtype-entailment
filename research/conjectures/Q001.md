# Q001 — Bounded essential terminal skeletons

Status: OPEN
Confidence: evidence
Introduced: 2026-09-05
Related: A008, A016, A017

## Claim

For every finite terminal witness instance arising in the current terminal formulation, if a successful witness exists then there is a successful witness whose path admits a decomposition with a computably bounded number of essential repeated blocks, where the bound depends only on the finite instance and the normalization preserves every required finite-border obligation.

The exact notion of "essential repeated block" must be chosen strongly enough to support the Presburger skeleton machinery and weak enough to admit a computable bound. This record therefore tracks the research hypothesis, not yet a final theorem statement.

## Why it matters

A positive theorem of this form would bridge A017 to A016: finitely many skeletons could be enumerated and each skeleton checked exactly with the existing arithmetic technique, yielding a route to a complete terminal decision procedure.

A strong counterexample family would instead show that fixed-skeleton coverage cannot be the completeness mechanism and would redirect effort toward saturation or certificates.

## Evidence for

- Flat terminal graphs already have a finite complete skeleton cover under the current arithmetic treatment.
- The complete normalized four-state terminal census has no unresolved cases and exhibits small witness bounds.
- Context-sensitive shortening rules eliminate several classes of inessential repetition.

## Evidence against / boundary cases

- State repetition alone is insufficient for deleting a loop: A017 contains a three-state obstruction.
- Border obligations can cross deletion boundaries, so ordinary finite-state pumping arguments do not directly apply.
- Abstract witness budgets already grow linearly in a known family, ruling out several naive small bounds.
