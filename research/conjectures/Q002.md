# Q002 - Agreement-profile domination for terminal words

Status: REFUTED
Confidence: explicit all-length counterexample; independent review pending
Introduced: 2026-09-05
Resolved: 2026-09-05
Related: A008, A009, A017, C034, C038, C041, C044, C046

## Resolution

C046 gives an eight-state terminal-final binary instance with exact witness
language `{1^(6a) 0 1^(6a) 0 : a>=1}`. Its unique shortest witness is
`11111101111110`, length 14; its minimum sufficient budget is seven, while
`k-2=6`. All 28 lower-budget splits are independently exhausted in E031.

Let w be that witness and G the five flags in the construction. Since G is
disjoint from A(w), any accepted v with A(v) subseteq A(w) also avoids G.
Every such v is therefore in the displayed language and has budget at least
seven. No replacement of ANY length meets the proposed budget.

The construction extends to `B=(r-1)(lcm(p_1,...,p_r)+1)` using
`k=1+sum_i(p_i+1)` states. Taking periods 1,...,m proves a superpolynomial
lower bound `B >= 2^(Omega(sqrt(k)))`. The original small-state census and
finite search outcomes are not contradicted; only their conjectured
extension is refuted. The new proof is written, not Lean-checked.

## Original claim (refuted)

Let M be a k-state partial deterministic automaton over the binary alphabet,
with terminal accepting states. For every boundary-safe accepted word w,
there exists a boundary-safe accepted word v such that

    A(v) subseteq A(w)
    word_budget(v) <= k-2.

A(x) is the full state-pair suffix-agreement profile, including diagonal and
empty-suffix agreements. C044's equivalence between this statement and the
terminal k-2 budget bound remains valid; both equivalent statements are false.

The maximal preserving flags remain useful:

    G_w = (Q x Q) minus A(w).

A low-budget witness for G_w is precisely a profile-dominated replacement.
The reformulation itself is not invalidated by the counterexample.

## Earlier established cases and finite evidence (retained)

For k=2,3,4, the bounded statement follows from the complete terminal census
C034. Flat terminal graphs remain decidable by the Presburger skeleton
procedure C033, but flatness does not imply this budget bound: C046 is flat.

Earlier maximal-profile samples were reported as:

- k=5: 1,134/1,134 high-budget targets normalized, zero caps;
- k=6: 403/403 normalized, zero caps;
- additional samples: k=5 492/492, k=6 425/425, k=7 163/163;
- separate adversarial runs: 1,741 k=5 targets and 1,568 k=6 targets, with
  intrinsic budgets as high as 11 and 12, and no counterexample found.

These were bounded falsification observations, not proofs. Their original
context is retained in the [profile note](../../notes/2026-09-05-profile-and-fractional-power.md).

## Failed stronger shortcuts and structural observations (retained)

The target word itself may have much larger budget than k-2. A replacement
need not be simple: the k=5 example `0100100 -> 010100` has a dominated
replacement of budget one, but its only simple accepted path `0000` is not
dominated. State repetition alone does not justify deletion. Source-local
viability plus a cyclic last-flag graph also does not imply YES (C042).

C041 still rewrites word budget as `min_m(m-1+g_m)`, where g_m is the gap
between the earliest and terminal occurrences of the suffix of length m.
A high-budget word has the earlier conjecture's simultaneous suffix staircase.
C046 shows that this staircase can survive in a shortest witnessing
word: it does not force the proposed shortening.

## Consequence

Do not use Q002 as the missing global bound or try to prove it unchanged.
Any general normalization bound must admit superpolynomial arithmetic
synchronization. A computable bounded-skeleton cover for general terminal
instances remains a possible route, but this counterexample neither proves
that cover nor rules out decidability.
