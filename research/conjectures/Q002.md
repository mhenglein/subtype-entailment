# Q002 — Agreement-profile domination for terminal words

Status: OPEN
Confidence: strong computational evidence
Introduced: 2026-09-05
Related: A008, A017, C034, C038, C041, C044

## Claim

Let M be a k-state partial deterministic automaton over the binary alphabet,
with terminal accepting states. For every boundary-safe accepted word `w`,
there exists a boundary-safe accepted word `v` such that

    A(v) subseteq A(w)

and

    word_budget(v) <= k-2,

where `A(x)` is the full state-pair suffix-agreement profile of `x`, including
diagonal and empty-suffix agreements.

## Why it matters

C044 proves that this statement is **equivalent** to the sharp terminal budget
bound: every positive k-state terminal-final BorderAvoid instance has a witness
with sufficient budget at most `k-2`.

Combined with the existing finite Strong Sandwich construction, that bound
would give a terminating exact decision for every terminal-final instance.
Through C031/C030 and the already-decided infinitely-extendable branch, this
would close the current route to normalized Problem 1.7 (subject to the
recorded proof status of those reductions).

The reformulation removes the arbitrary input flag relation. Given `w`, the
hardest flag relation preserving it is exactly

    G_w = (Q x Q) \ A(w).

A low-budget witness for `G_w` is precisely a profile-dominated replacement.

## Established cases

- `k<=4`: follows from the complete terminal census C034 by applying it to the
  maximal preserving flags `G_w`.
- Flat terminal candidate graphs are independently decidable by the A016
  Presburger skeleton procedure, but that does not prove the `k-2` bound.

## Evidence for

Adversarial searches choose a terminal transition table, a high-intrinsic-budget
accepted target word, then add **every** flag preserving that target. Exact
finite Strong Sandwich searches exhaust every budget split through `k-2`.

Completed profile-normalization samples include:

- k=5: 1,134/1,134 high-budget targets normalized, zero caps;
- k=6: 403/403 normalized, zero caps;
- additional runs: k=5 492/492, k=6 425/425, k=7 163/163.

Separate maximal-flag adversarial runs tested 1,741 k=5 targets and 1,568 k=6
targets, with target intrinsic budgets as high as 11 and 12 respectively, and
found no counterexample.

This is falsification evidence, not a proof.

## Boundary cases / failed strengthenings

- The target word itself can have arbitrarily larger budget than `k-2`; the
  claim requires a replacement word.
- The replacement need not be a simple accepting path. A k=5 example has
  target `0100100`, profile-dominated replacement `010100` of budget one, but
  its only simple accepting path `0000` is not profile-dominated.
- Arbitrary source-local viability plus a cyclic last-flag pair graph does not
  imply existence (C042). The conjecture uses the much stronger fact that all
  allowed pairs arise together in the profile of one accepted word.
- State repetition alone does not justify loop deletion. Any proof must use
  simultaneous agreement structure, not ordinary DFA pumping.

## Structural leverage

C041 rewrites per-word budget as

    min_m (m-1 + g_m),

where `g_m` is the start-gap between the earliest copy of the terminal
length-m suffix and its terminal copy. Therefore a counterexample to Q002
would have a shortest profile-minimal word satisfying

    g_m >= k-m   for every 1<=m<=k-1.

This creates a nested staircase of repeated terminal suffixes. C036/C038 bound
the aligned state behavior and repetition exponent of each comparison, while
C043 puts every proper violation in bounded fractional-power form.

## Next discriminating step

Work with a shortest accepted word `w` that is minimal under agreement-profile
containment and violates the claimed budget. Use the simultaneous suffix
staircase to derive either:

1. a loop modification producing an accepted `v` with `A(v) subseteq A(w)`;
   or
2. a finite obstruction showing why all such modifications introduce a new
   agreement pair.

Before promoting any shortening lemma, search maximal-profile instances for its
smallest counterexample. Preserve exact `UNKNOWN` semantics for capped finite
automaton searches.
