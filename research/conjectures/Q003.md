# Q003 - Strict agreement-profile descent

Status: REFUTED
Confidence: explicit all-length counterexample; independent review pending
Introduced: 2026-09-05
Resolved: 2026-09-05
Related: Q002, A008, A017, C041, C044, C045, C046, C047

## Resolution

C046's eight-state automaton has shortest witness `11111101111110`, length
14 and budget seven, greater than k-2=6. Its five flags avoid A(w). Any
profile-dominated accepted v would also avoid these flags, but every such
word has the form `1^(6a) 0 1^(6a) 0`, a>=1. No strictly shorter v exists.

This is not just failure of a particular loop-deletion rule. No shorter
accepted word with a dominated profile exists by ANY construction. The
all-length language proof is accompanied by all 32,767 binary words through
length 14, both word predicates, all lower-budget graph searches, and an
exact regular-countermodel check for the subtype realization. Independent
review is requested; no Lean formalization is claimed.

## Original claim (refuted)

For every k-state terminal-final binary DFA and every accepted word w with

    word_budget(w) > k-2,

there exists a strictly shorter accepted v with A(v) subseteq A(w), where A
is the full state-pair suffix-agreement profile. Repeated descent would have
proved Q002. C046 refutes both statements, even with k>=2 explicitly imposed.

## Earlier bounded evidence (retained)

The previous scan enumerated accepted words through length 12 in random
terminal transition tables:

- k=5: 1,200 tables, 4,701 high-budget targets;
- k=6: 900 tables, 1,048 targets;
- k=7: 500 tables, 271 targets.

All 6,020 tested targets had a shorter dominated word; the maximum target
budget was eight. The new k=8, length-14 counterexample lies outside that
coverage. These results remain finite observations, not proof of Q003.
The original runner is [check_profile_descent.py](../../experiments/check_profile_descent.py).

## Loop-deletion observations and their limits

C045's local unbordered-gap lemma is not contradicted. Single-loop descent
was already refuted by `1111011110 -> 110110`, and by a five-state example
`11101110 -> 110110`. A later 1,643-target scan found no case needing more
than two simultaneous deletions. C047 now supplies an explicit family that
needs exactly r deletions on 2r+1 states: `(1110)^r -> (10)^r`. For r=3,
`111011101110 -> 101010` needs three deletions.

That family rules out every constant deletion count independent of k. It
still has a shorter dominated word. The different-period family C046 is
stronger in a different direction: its shortest witness cannot be shortened
at all. Do not confuse these two obstructions.

The earlier approach and its proof dependencies are retained in the
[unbordered-gap note](../../notes/2026-09-05-unbordered-gap-normalization.md).

## Consequence

A general argument needs arithmetic base cases, not automatic descent above
k-2. The fixed-skeleton solver handles the modular family exactly. Its
extension to a complete effective cover for nonflat terminal instances is
still unproved. No unrestricted decision follows from this counterexample.
