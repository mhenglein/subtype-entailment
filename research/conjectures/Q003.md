## Resolution

C046's eight-state automaton has shortest witness `11111101111110`, length
14 and budget seven, greater than k-2=6. Its five flags avoid A(w). Any
profile-dominated accepted v would also avoid these flags, but every such
word has the form `1^(6a) 0 1^(6a) 0`, a>=1. No strictly shorter v exists.

This is not just failure of a particular loop-deletion rule. No shorter
accepted word with a dominated profile exists by any construction. The
all-length language proof is accompanied by the bounded complete checks in
the modular-synchronization regression. Independent review is requested; no
Lean formalization is claimed.

## Original claim (refuted)

For every k-state terminal-final binary DFA and every accepted word w with

    word_budget(w) > k-2,

there exists a strictly shorter accepted v with A(v) subseteq A(w), where A
is the full state-pair suffix-agreement profile. Repeated descent would have
proved Q002. C046 refutes both statements.

## Earlier bounded evidence retained

The previous scan enumerated accepted words through length 12 in random
terminal transition tables for k=5,6,7. All 6,020 tested high-budget targets
had a shorter dominated word. The new k=8, length-14 counterexample lies
outside that coverage. Those results remain finite observations, not proofs.

C045's local unbordered-gap lemma is not contradicted. C047 separately gives
an explicit family requiring an unbounded number of simultaneous loop
deletions, while still admitting a shorter dominated word. Do not conflate
that deletion-count obstruction with C046's stronger no-shorter-word example.

## Consequence

A general argument needs arithmetic base cases, not automatic descent above
k-2. Symbolic skeleton methods handle the modular family itself, but a complete
effective cover for arbitrary nonflat terminal instances remains unproved.
