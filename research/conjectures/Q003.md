## Resolution — C046

The eight-state terminal automaton in C046 has unique shortest witness
`11111101111110`, of length 14 and budget 7>k-2=6. Every profile-dominated
accepted replacement would also be a witness of the same five-flag instance.
Its exact witness language is `{(1^(6t)0)^2 : t>=1}`, so no strictly shorter
profile-dominated replacement exists, regardless of how many loops are deleted
or whether the replacement is obtained by loop deletion at all.

E029 checks all 32,767 binary words through length 14; none of the seven
shorter accepted words has a dominated profile. It also exhausts all 28
Strong Sandwich splits through budget six. The language and budget formulas
have written proofs, pending independent review and not Lean-checked.
No eight-state minimality claim is made.

## Original claim

Let `M` be a `k`-state partial deterministic automaton over the binary alphabet
with terminal accepting states. For every accepted word `w` with

    word_budget(w) > k-2,

there exists a strictly shorter accepted word `v` such that

    A(v) subseteq A(w),

where `A` is the full state-pair suffix-agreement profile.

## Why it matters

Repeated application must terminate. The terminal word at which descent stops
has budget at most `k-2`, so Q003 implies Q002. Through C044 this would prove
the sharp terminal budget bound and make the existing Strong Sandwich search
an exact terminal decision procedure.

Q003 is stronger than Q002 because Q002 does not require the replacement to be
shorter than the supplied target.

## Historical evidence

A deterministic bounded search enumerated all accepted words through length 12
for random terminal transition tables and tested every high-budget target:

- k=5: 1,200 tables, 4,701 high-budget targets;
- k=6: 900 tables, 1,048 targets;
- k=7: 500 tables, 271 targets.

The maximum target budget observed was eight. Every one of the 6,020 targets
had a strictly shorter accepted word with dominated profile. This is
falsification evidence only.

## Known structure and failed strengthenings

C045 proves one local descent rule: an unbordered repeated terminal block can
have its intervening state loop deleted when the shortened word has a unique
terminal suffix one letter longer than the block.

A single-loop version of Q003 is false. The six-state target `1111011110` in
the C045 note has no profile-safe single equal-state loop deletion, but deleting
two disjoint loops gives `110110` with dominated profile. A five-state example
`11101110 -> 110110` behaves similarly.

A bounded run over 1,643 high-budget targets through eight states found no
case needing more than two simultaneous loop deletions, but this is not a
claim that two always suffice.

## Historical next discriminating step

Try to prove a finite multi-pump descent lemma rather than a single-loop lemma.
Anderson et al.'s unbordered-word argument is relevant because it combines
pump-downs on separated regions; however C039 shows that preserving state
colour is the nontrivial part. For Q003 the required invariant is weaker:
new borders are harmless whenever their state pair already belongs to `A(w)`.

Before strengthening Q003 to a bounded number of loop deletions, search for a
small counterexample requiring three or more. Preserve exact distinction
between bounded falsification and an all-state proof.
