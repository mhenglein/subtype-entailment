C065 proves a complete decision for EVERY binary terminal instance with at most
one flag. Removing source returns is existentially sound; terminality makes a
forbidden period ending at a fixed target unique. Finite transition-monoid
fibres give rational accepted-word and period-incidence length series. Their
difference counts witnesses and has a bounded numerator degree, yielding the
explicit bound `[k(k+1)/2]*(k+1)^k+k-2` for k>=2. The same argument handles a
certified single-source target antichain. It does not invoke the unproved
single-source root pump, or assume a regular witness language.

C066 refutes naive extension to two interacting targets: an acyclic instance
has candidate series 2*z^4 and incidence series 2*z^4 but a witness series z^4.
Higher joint-period counts of the SAME word are needed; counts from independently
chosen words cannot replace them. A one-source theorem still would not justify
source-wise composition for arbitrary flags. An opposing undecidability reduction
in the exact terminal fragment must use more than one flag, but that is not
an undecidability claim for multiple flags.
