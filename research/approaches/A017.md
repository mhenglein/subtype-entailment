# A017: Bound essential repeated blocks

Current status, owner, and next step: [research tracker](../README.md). Metadata lives in [approaches.json](../approaches.json).

## Question and scope

Does every successful BorderAvoid instance have a witness describable by a fixed repetition skeleton whose size is effectively bounded by the instance encoding?

## Attempt history

### 2026-09-04 — Research-plan proposal

Recorded by Astra from the ten-angle outline. No decomposition theorem or block-deletion experiment has been attempted here.

First study words x u^k y, then two interacting repeated blocks. Record the transition effect of each block and all new borders crossing a deletion boundary. Test proposed deletion hypotheses against the independent witness predicate and minimize failures.

Investigate the transition monoid and [factorization forests](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.5130) as ways to organize repeated behavior. The theorem supplies bounded-height decompositions for finite semigroups. It does not itself bound the number of relevant blocks or preserve our endpoint flags.

### 2026-09-04 — Exact loop-deletion obligation

Owner: Astra (`context_cut`).

An interior deletion between equal DFA states preserves the run and endpoint.
To preserve witnesses, every newly agreeing suffix pair that crosses the
join must lift to an agreeing pair before deletion. A written sufficient
condition makes this explicit. The three-state `001 -> 00` example shows
why repeated states alone fail.

This is a precise local proof obligation, not a finite skeleton theorem.
It supplies no bound on the number of essential blocks. See
[C019](../claims.md#c019) and the
[structure note](../../notes/2026-09-04-minimal-witness-structure.md).

### 2026-09-05 - Bounded exponent inside every terminal violation

C038 uses C036 to show that if an accepted terminal word has a proper suffix
agreement at positions i<j, with d=j-i and source-suffix length N, then
`floor(N/d) <= k-1`. Equivalently, the source suffix is `y^t r` for the gap
word `y`, a prefix `r` of `y`, and `t<=k-1`. The bound is sharp on unary
terminal paths.

This controls repetitions *inside* an arbitrarily long synchronized-pair
comparison, including cyclic residuals left UNKNOWN by C037. It does not bound
the gap length d, so it is not a word-length, budget, or finite-skeleton theorem.
The remaining problem can be viewed as bounded-use equality patterns under
regular path control. Related backreference-universality results are recorded
only as context; their languages are more general, so no undecidability
transfer is claimed.

E026 checks 2,000 seeded terminal automata, 11,631 accepted words, and 11,918
flagged agreements, plus the sharp family for k=2..8. See the
[proof](../../notes/2026-09-05-terminal-period-bound.md),
[experiment](../../experiments/check_terminal_period_bound.py), and
[result](../../results/terminal-period-bound.json).

### 2026-09-05 — Profile descent fails; finite symbolic synchronization survives

Recorded by ChatGPT on `research/terminal-copy-synchronization`, base `2c6b4aac`.
The Q002/Q003 route aimed to replace arbitrary terminal witnesses with
profile-dominated words of budget at most k-2. C046 refutes it: an eight-state
flat terminal instance has exact budget seven and no shorter profile-dominated
replacement of its length-14 shortest witness. Its LCM generalization forces
super-polynomial budgets even though the number of unary skeleton blocks is
small. E029 supplies exact lower-budget certificates and separate finite checks.

C047-C048 show a richer controlled phenomenon: terminal flags can force
arbitrary regular blocks to be identical, and a fixed-length binary encoding
preserves every flagged comparison. The constructed subclass has an exact
finite-product solution when its component DFAs are supplied. The same
construction gives the C049 top-free hardness route through C030.

Outcome: Q002 and Q003 refuted; Q001 remains open. Written proofs with
reproduction are pending independent review, not Lean-checked. Neither large
exponents nor PSPACE-hardness refute the existence of a computable skeleton
bound. No complete normalization for general nonflat inputs is established.

Next: seek an effective finite cover by skeletons or synchronized-block
contexts that retains regular constraints and all joins. Validate proposed
preservation rules on the LCM and regular-copy families; do not revive a
polynomial budget threshold or infer an arbitrary input decomposition from
the constructed product-solvable subclass.

## Result and limits

Success requires an effective normalization theorem plus correct preservation of every witness obligation. Combining such a theorem with a checked implementation of [A016](A016.md) could give a decision algorithm. Equality of transition effects alone is insufficient because deletion can create new borders.
