# Terminal one-use backreferences: literature boundary for Problem 1.7

Date: 2026-09-08.

**Status: literature/translation audit, not a new decidability or hardness theorem.**

## 1. Exact correspondence on the bad-language side

After terminal boundary normalization, a non-overlapping BorderAvoid violation
has the form

    w = A-part · z · C-part · z,

with regular path constraints on the prefix, captured word, middle path, and
flagged source/target states. The second occurrence of `z` ends the candidate.
This is a finite union of state-constrained **terminal 1-use backreference**
branches, i.e. the canonical shape

    A (B)_x C \x

with `D=epsilon` in the usual `A(B)_x C \x D` notation.

Proper overlap is even more constrained: terminal nonmerging gives the bounded
period form `x(uv)^e u`, `1<=e<=k-1`. Thus the unresolved containment problem is
not arbitrary backreference universality.

## 2. ICALP 2026 gives membership, not containment

Soh Kumabe and Yuya Uezato, *On the Complexity of the Matching Problem of
Regular Expressions with Backreferences*, ICALP 2026, prove that every 1-use
REWB can be normalized to a finite union of branches

    A_i (B_i)_x C_i \x D_i

plus a regular residual, and give a near-linear algorithm for matching one input
word against such a branch. Their algorithm uses suffix trees, transition
monoids, factorization-forest machinery and periodicity.

This is highly relevant technically, but the quantifiers differ from Subtail.
We need to decide whether **every** word of a supplied regular candidate language
belongs to a finite union of terminal-copy/overlap bad languages, or equivalently
whether there exists a regular candidate outside that union. A fast per-word
membership procedure does not imply that regular-language containment is
decidable, and the paper does not claim such a theorem.

Primary source:
https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.135

## 3. Ordinary unbordered regular languages are decidable

Anderson, Loftus, Rampersad, Santean and Shallit, *Detecting palindromes,
patterns and borders in regular languages* (Information and Computation 207,
2009), Theorem 58, prove that an n-state NFA accepting an unbordered word accepts
one of length at most `6n+1`. Hence ordinary regular-language containment in the
language of bordered words is decidable.

The proof shortens two central loops, obtains borders of the shortened words,
and uses conjugacy to force a short border of the original word. For our
state-coloured target relation, the word equality survives but the suffix-start
state can move forward. `notes/2026-09-05-source-border-anderson.md` therefore
needs forward closure of the target set to preserve the colour. The original
proof does not silently remove that hypothesis.

Primary source:
https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.3183

## 4. Why FC[REG]-CQ universality is not yet an undecidability reduction

Thompson and Freydenberger, *Languages Generated by Conjunctive Query Fragments
of FC[REG]* (Theory of Computing Systems, 2024), build invalid-computation
languages as finite unions of regularly typed patterns and obtain strong
undecidability/non-semi-decidability results for richer query fragments.

The construction is an important adversarial template, but its error patterns
do not have the terminal-copy restriction above. The tape-error gadgets use
multiple regularly typed unary variables, arbitrary prefix/suffix contexts, and
repeated variables whose second occurrence can be followed by further material.
A local error between two middle configurations is therefore expressible there
without making its copied occurrence extend to the end of the whole history.

BorderAvoid has the opposite anchoring restriction: in a violation, the target
copy is the suffix from the target cut to the **end of the candidate**. Reversing
the word merely moves this global anchor to the beginning; it does not make an
arbitrary internal local comparison available. Consequently an invalid-
computation reduction must explicitly solve this anchoring/quantifier problem.
Citing general FC[REG]-CQ universality is not sufficient.

Primary source:
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00224-024-10198-4

## 5. Exact open language-theoretic question

No source found in this audit directly settles:

> Given a regular language R, is R contained in a finite union of
> state-constrained terminal one-copy languages `A(B)_x C \x` plus the bounded
> terminal-overlap languages forced by nonmerging?

This is the cleanest external formulation of the terminal BorderAvoid frontier.
It is strictly more structured than general 1-use REWB universality and more
structured than the FC[REG] invalid-computation patterns, while strictly richer
than ordinary bordered-word containment.

## 6. Research consequences

Two directions remain credible:

1. **Decidability:** lift transition-monoid/factorization-forest techniques from
   per-word 1-use matching to regular-language containment while retaining the
   state colour at the terminal copy start. The arithmetic cut-selector work is
   a concrete finite fragment of such a lift.
2. **Undecidability:** design a computation encoding in which every local error
   can be exposed at the global terminal anchor, without requiring a nonregular
   list of all history rotations/copies. This anchoring step is the missing
   reduction gadget.

Any future literature claim should state which of these quantifier/anchoring
obligations it actually resolves.
