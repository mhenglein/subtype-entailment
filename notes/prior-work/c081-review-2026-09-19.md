This note compares the checked C081 theorem with the direct non-structural

## September 20 correction: a restricted upper-bound transfer

The initial review below did not supply a semantic map to NP99. A subsequent
ordinary proof now supplies one for the **input fragment without bottom
constants**, while preserving that gap for full C081. The
[extension study, T1–T2](../2026-09-19-post-c081-extensions.md) proves the map and
its polynomial size; an [independent readback](../2026-09-19-post-c081-extension-readback.md)
accepted both directions. Product naming plus a fresh `omega=omega×omega`
and guards `z<=omega` force all target names to be top-free. NP99's exact
restricted grammar accepts this target. Its Theorem 14 gives a PSPACE upper
bound for the source arbitrary-tree fragment. The study's separate regular
countermodel proof transfers this upper bound to regular source valuations.

This is not identity translation: `x=x×x, x<=y` entails `y<=x` in the top-free
domain but has the explicit-top countermodel `x=Omega, y=top`. Nor is it a
finite-tree reduction: the omega equation has no finite solution. The input
restriction excludes bottom literals and normalized denials, not bottom values
in source valuations. No lower bound transfers in this direction, and no
PSPACE running-time/space claim is made for the existing C081 implementation.

Consequently, decidability of the bottom-free arbitrary-tree input fragment
must be credited to the earlier result via this map. Candidate novelty remains
the full bottom/product theorem and the specific checked terminal construction,
subject to the original search and priority limits. Later references below to
an entirely missing semantic transfer are historical and superseded by this
restricted result; a full-C081 transfer remains unproved. The reviewed R1–R4
argument also now discharges the ordinary regular-countermodel obligation;
finite-tree transfer remains false in general, and no new Lean extension
theorem is claimed.

## Review result

The inspected literature establishes close prior art for three parts of the
current result:

1. finite tree-address encodings of subtype values and path-wise countermodels;
2. automata and cap-language reductions for non-structural entailment; and
3. shared periodic words and hidden equations of the form `pi nu = mu pi`.

The closest exact source is Niehren–Priesnitz 2003 (NP03). Its Theorems 1, 2,
and 4 characterize full-signature NSSE with one covariant constructor by
universality of restricted cap expressions/automata, for finite, regular, and
possibly infinite trees. That is established prior art for the general
automata-and-word-equation viewpoint. It does not, by itself, identify the
current top-free product-node semantics with their model, nor does it supply
the current terminal first-return/rank-query compiler.

Relative to the inspected source set, the following is a **candidate-new
checked result**:

> A total executable Boolean, with a kernel-checked correctness theorem, for
> finite syntax over variables, bottom, and one covariant binary product, where
> valuations range over all prefix-closed product-node trees, including
> nonregular infinite trees.

That wording is deliberately narrower than “first decidable NSSE result.” The
review did not prove that the top-free semantics cannot be encoded into an
earlier full-signature theorem, did not inspect every forward citation, and did
not locate a complete archival proof behind the BPR19 slide announcement. The
novelty claim should therefore remain **candidate / bounded review** until the
paper compares an explicit two-way semantic translation or documents a wider
literature search.

## Exact C081 object

The exact checked input and semantic domain are visible in the following
declarations:

- `lean/Subtail/ExpressionNormalization.lean:12-29` defines finite
  expressions `var`, `bot`, and `prod`, finite constraint input `G`, evaluation,
  model satisfaction, and the query entailment predicate.
- `lean/Subtail/TreeSemantics.lean:13-30` defines a tree as an arbitrary
  predicate on binary words with a prefix-closure proof. Product nodes are the
  predicate's true addresses; absent children are bottom leaves. There is no
  finite-state, regularity, or depth bound in this type.
- `lean/Subtail/TreeSemantics.lean:120-142` defines the normalized finite
  clauses `leq`, `upper`, `lower`, and `denial`, with covariant product order.
- `lean/Subtail/ExpressionNormalization.lean:295-343` proves the finite
  normalization and the two-way model extension/restriction transfer.
- `lean/Subtail/EntailmentDecision.lean:18-32` defines and proves the complete
  normalized Boolean backend; lines 46-76 add proved cheap cases; lines 80-93
  compose the expression-level theorem.

The target is therefore not “finite types.” It is finite *syntax* with an
arbitrary-tree valuation domain. C081 also does not contain an explicit `top`
constructor or a top-labelled leaf. The full binary product tree is the greatest
element under the product-node inclusion order, but it is not the same labelled
tree as the explicit `>` leaf in the NP03/Schubert models. This distinction is
the main semantic boundary in the novelty comparison.

The theorem being compared is:

```lean
Subtail.EntailmentDecision.entailsCheck_correct
  (G : Input n) (s t : Expr n) :
  entailsCheck G s t = true ↔ ExprEntails G s t
```

The caller supplies no satisfiability, finite-model, regular-model,
witness-bound, absorption, `U/F`, or C030 premise. The diagnostic timeout cap
is an experiment limit and is not part of the formal Boolean.

### Signature and variance conclusion

The direct lineage already covers one covariant constructor, binary arity, and
both finite and regular/infinite domains. It also contains the relevant word
equation and periodic continuation ideas. Therefore the paper must not present
“binary product,” “finite automata,” “periodic words,” or “hidden word
equations” as new in isolation.

The potentially distinguishing semantic axis is the valuation domain: C081 has
only bottom/product trees represented by prefix-closed product-node sets, with
the full infinite product tree as greatest element; NP99, NP03, SU06 and SCH21
use explicit bottom/top-labelled tree signatures. A result that is new for this
top-free model may still be reducible to the full-signature results. That
reduction has not been supplied or ruled out here.

The other potentially distinguishing axis is the exact checked algorithm. C081
combines a finite expression normalizer, a summary automaton with ordered
endpoint flags, a strong-absorption theorem for the realized instance, an
infinite safe-continuation decision, and a terminal first-return/rank-query
compiler that preserves the whole conjunction and shared generators. The
inspected sources provide neighboring cap automata, safety languages, and
periodic selections, but no exact theorem matching all of these interfaces.
This is a candidate algorithmic novelty claim, not yet a literature absence
claim.

## Backward citation trace from NP03

NP03's journal PDF gives the following backward chain. The page numbers below
refer to the author manuscript `_02`; the TACS `_01` precursor uses different
theorem numbers.

1. **HR97 and HR98, references [14] and [15], journal pp. 43–44.** These are
   the structural coNP result and recursive structural PSPACE result, plus the
   non-structural PSPACE-hardness baseline cited in the introduction. They
   establish complexity context, not a C081 semantic transfer.
2. **NP99, reference [16].** NP03 explicitly extends the earlier restricted
   P-automata work. NP99's Theorem 14 is the finite-automaton universality
   result for the restricted ambient bottom/top/product language.
3. **Makanin and Plandowski, references [17] and [18].** These are word-equation
   foundations used to explain why cap membership creates the hard shared-word
   equation. NP03's Lemma 1 and Corollary 2 give the exact local use.
4. **Su et al. and Treinen, references [21] and [23].** These are the
   constrained-tree-automata and equality-test background. NP03 explicitly
   separates its word-automata method from the tree-automata approach because
   the latter's emptiness problem is undecidable in the cited setting (journal
   PDF pp. 2 and 43–45).
5. **The TACS 2001 precursor, reference [24].** It is the earlier version of
   the same cap-automata characterization. The journal version says that the
   omitted proofs, especially completeness, are supplied there in expanded
   form; do not merge theorem numbers across `_01` and `_02`.

This chain makes the following prior art secure: the automata reduction and the
word-equation obstacle are not new starting points. It does not settle whether
the checked top-free summary/terminal construction is equivalent to, an
instance of, or independent of the restricted cap-automaton universality
problem.

## Inherited, candidate-new, and unresolved components

### Inherited or clearly adjacent

- Tree values addressed by words with prefix-closed domains, and separating
  non-entailment at a finite address, are standard in the direct lineage and in
  the memo's own path semantics.
- Covariant product decomposition and finite normalization/flattening are
  standard techniques. C081's exact extension/restriction proof is checked,
  but its broad idea is not claimed as unprecedented.
- P-automata/cap expressions, periodic continuation, repeated words and hidden
  equations predate C081. NP03's Lemma 1 is the clearest exact comparator.
- The distinction between finite, regular and arbitrary/infinite values is
  explicit in NP03 and SU06; the paper must preserve that distinction.

### Candidate-new relative to the inspected primary sources

- A total executable Boolean and kernel-checked correctness theorem for the
  exact top-free arbitrary-tree product-node semantics.
- The realized finite summary state with ordered endpoint flags and strong
  absorption, followed by the safe infinite/terminal split, as a complete
  composition rather than a characterization conditional on a separate
  universality oracle.
- The terminal first-return/rank-query compiler's preservation of one shared
  generator across a whole conjunction of interval constraints, with proved
  finite iteration and semantic unwind. No inspected source states this exact
  compiler theorem.
- The expression-level normalizer's two-way model extension/restriction and
  query transfer for duplicate nested product occurrences and bottom literals,
  integrated with the complete backend.

These are strongest as “new checked construction for the stated semantics”
until a source-to-source equivalence or prior implementation search is done.

### Unresolved

- **Top-free/full-signature equivalence.** Is every C081 instance reducible to
  NP03/NP99 without allowing explicit top-labelled assignments, and conversely?
  The current repository has no such theorem.
- **Lower-bound transfer.** Does the HR98/NP99 PSPACE-hardness construction
  survive when variables range only over bottom/product trees? The existing
  citation does not prove this. A top-free reduction is needed before citing a
  C081 lower bound.
- **Finite/regular restriction.** C081 decides a larger valuation domain than
  finite or regular values. The theorem does not automatically imply a separate
  finite-tree or regular-tree entailment theorem, because changing the model
  class can change entailment.
- **BPR19 coverage.** The announced finite periodic selection may subsume part
  of the C081 continuation machinery, but no full proof or exact map was found.
- **Terminal/compiler priority.** The bounded search found no exact matching
  theorem, but this is not a proof that none exists, especially in theses,
  unpublished notes, or sources not indexed by the direct title lineage.
- **Complexity.** No inspected source or C081 record presently establishes a
  useful complexity class for the total checked Boolean. The old C080 U/F
  numerical claims remain separate.

## Recommended paper wording

The current paper can safely say:

> We give a kernel-checked total decision procedure for the top-free,
> covariant-binary-product subtype-entailment problem over arbitrary
> prefix-closed trees. The proof combines a finite normalization, a finite
> summary bridge, a safe infinite-continuation decision, and a finite terminal
> first-return decision. The construction is compared with the full-signature
> cap-automata and word-equation characterizations of Niehren and Priesnitz;
> those works provide close prior art but do not supply the semantic transfer
> or the checked terminal construction used here.

It should not say “first decidable NSSE,” “first use of word equations,”
“NP03 left this exact top-free problem open” without a transfer analysis, or
“C081 proves PSPACE membership.” The paper should include a table like the one
above and state that the novelty conclusion is based on a bounded source review.

## Best next novelty experiment

Construct an explicit semantic translation for the nearest benchmark:

1. define the NP03 binary full-signature model and its top-free submodel;
2. map a C081 product-node tree to a labelled tree and prove order
   preservation/reflection on the image;
3. test whether NP03's safety/cap construction preserves the image under every
   normalized C081 clause; and
4. either obtain a two-way reduction or record the precise obstruction.

This experiment would clarify whether C081 is semantically equivalent to an
NP03 cap-universality instance or whether the top-free domain or terminal
compiler separates it. NP03 cap universality is a characterization of the
full-signature problem, not an established decision theorem, so a reduction
there would establish a formulation equivalence or a reduction to an open
problem; it would not establish decidability or show that C081 is an effective
specialization of prior solved work. A transfer to the decidable restricted
class studied by NP99 would require a proof that every normalized C081 image
satisfies NP99's restricted hypotheses. Exact transfer of the HR98/NP99 lower
bounds to C081 also remains unresolved.
