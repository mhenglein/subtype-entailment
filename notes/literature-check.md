# Bounded literature check: Problem 1.7

Checked 2026-09-04 against the local memo sections 1.3 and 6–8 and the public source copies below. The memo's target is the semantic top-free fragment with `bottom` and one covariant binary product `×`, represented by prefix-closed path sets over `{0,1}`. It allows finite and infinite trees. This is narrower than the historical non-structural subtype-entailment problem.

## Status

The historical open-problem statements remain useful corroboration: the [TLCA open-problem page](https://tlca.di.unito.it/opltlca/opltlcasu23.html) records Rehof's 1996 problem and says the Horn entailment question was open after the PSPACE-hardness and first-order undecidability results. The page notes that its status is based on a 2014 snapshot, so it is not proof of a 2026 status. A later 2016 thesis by Dolan also describes non-structural entailment for the non-extensible semantics as still open ([thesis](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/~nr/cs257/archive/stephen-dolan/thesis.pdf)). The bounded search found no surviving published result that directly addresses the memo's semantic fragment `{bottom, ×}`.

## Useful adjacent technique

The 2025 ICALP paper by Bohn, Li, Löding, and Schewe, [Saturation Problems for Families of Automata](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol334-icalp2025/html/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.146/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.146.html), develops saturation and period-language tools for finite deterministic automata on prefixes and periods (FDFAs/FDWAs), including closure under powers, roots, and conjugation. This is adjacent, not a direct solution: the memo's `BorderAvoid` condition quantifies over all finite-prefix borders and couples two endpoint classes through `G`, whereas ordinary omega-automaton saturation handles lasso-style prefix/period pairs. A focused experiment is to derive the period languages of small sep instances and test whether the intersection-shaped `G` relation forces power/root/conjugation closure. If it does, an FDFA-style saturation reduction may be possible; if it fails, the smallest counterexample should clarify which extra invariant a proof needs.

## Implication for the next proof/experiment step

Use the Niehren–Priesnitz cap/word-equation lens to formalize each `G`-flagged border as a regular-constrained equation `πν = μπ`, while preserving the memo's `Fin` and `Bad` endpoint conditions. Then test whether the top-free restrictions (prefix closure, no top label, and the special intersection `G`) make this equation family substantially simpler. Any PSPACE-hardness claim should first exhibit a top-free replacement for the ambient-`top` mismatch used by Henglein–Rehof.
