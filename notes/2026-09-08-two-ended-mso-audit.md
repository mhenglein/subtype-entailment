# Two-ended MSO audit: why the naive Rabin shortcut does not apply

Date: 2026-09-08. Workstream: A017 / direct-semantics audit. Base main: `99133037a60f65c8e0fab5ed9299524fbd95043e`.

**Status: scope/falsification note. This is not an undecidability proof for Problem 1.7.**

## 1. The tempting argument

The top-free semantics identifies each type variable with an arbitrary prefix-closed set `X_alpha subseteq {0,1}*`, and subtyping is inclusion. It is therefore tempting to quantify one monadic set variable per type variable in the full binary tree and invoke Rabin's decidability of S2S.

The variable-inclusion and bottom clauses are local in that view. If the product clauses were also local with respect to the ordinary binary-tree successors, non-entailment would indeed look like a direct monadic second-order satisfiability question.

## 2. The orientation mismatch

The normalized product constraints do not use the same end of a word as prefix closure.

In the repository's exact bridge conventions, a lower product clause contributes

    a . L(source) subseteq L(target),

while an upper product clause contributes the corresponding left quotient

    L(source) / a subseteq L(target).

Thus product navigation acts by **prepending/removing the leading symbol** of the path word.

By contrast, prefix closure of a path set is ordinary word-prefix closure. On the standard full binary tree whose successors are

    w -> w0,   w -> w1,

that closure is ancestor closure obtained by deleting symbols at the **right-hand end**.

So an exact direct tree encoding would need, in addition to the ordinary right successors, a cross-end operation such as

    s_0(w) = 0w.

This operation is not one of Rabin's ordinary binary-tree successors.

## 3. Why one cannot simply add the missing operation

Classical automata/MSO literature shows that this is not an innocuous definitional extension. The weak monadic second-order theory of the full binary tree becomes undecidable after adding the left-prefix function `s(w)=0w` (equivalently, enough structure is available to interpret an infinite grid). Equal-level extensions give related undecidability phenomena.

Therefore a proof of Problem 1.7 by 'translate the normalized clauses to S2S and invoke Rabin' would have a genuine missing definability step. Treating left-prefix as though it were an ordinary tree successor silently changes the structure to one outside the decidable S2S theorem.

This observation is consistent with the repository's earlier proof audit, which already cautioned that claims that the MSO route was closed were only heuristic. The present note supplies the precise obstruction to the **naive** S2S formulation; it does not rule out every more sophisticated logical encoding.

## 4. No undecidability transfer is claimed

The undecidability of MSO over the enriched two-ended tree does **not** imply that the tiny Horn-like fragment generated by normalized subtype constraints is undecidable. Constraint satisfiability itself is already decidable by the repository's pushdown/least-closure argument, showing that the fragment is far weaker than arbitrary MSO over that enriched structure.

Likewise, no tiling, PCP, or machine-computation reduction into subtype entailment is supplied here. Such a reduction would have to use only the four normalized constraint forms and preserve the exact arbitrary-tree semantics.

## 5. Structural interpretation

The failed shortcut is nevertheless informative. A candidate counterexample path is controlled from both ends:

* prefix-closed semantic seeds expose its ordinary prefixes; and
* product propagation pushes/pops symbols at the opposite end.

The BorderAvoid construction is therefore not merely a complicated presentation of a one-ended regular-tree problem. Its suffix/border comparisons are the finite-word shadow of this two-ended interaction.

## 6. Handoff

Two honest routes remain:

1. **decidability:** exploit restrictions of the Horn/pushdown fragment to obtain a finite or well-founded normalization despite the two-ended interaction; or
2. **undecidability:** show that those restrictions still suffice to encode a genuine grid/PCP computation, with both reduction directions checked.

The current A017 terminal reformulation gives a sharper decidability target: after boundary normalization, decide whether a regular candidate language escapes a finite union of state-constrained one-copy terminal patterns and bounded-period overlap patterns. This avoids invoking unrestricted two-ended MSO.

Literature pointer used for the obstruction: the standard automata-on-infinite-objects treatment records undecidability of weak MSO for the binary tree extended by the left-prefix map `w -> 0w`. This citation is context for the failed shortcut, not a theorem about Subtail's constraint fragment.
